Active Learning Plan¶

How multioutreg will choose the next points to evaluate, so that a surrogate reaches a target accuracy (or finds a good design) with fewer runs of an expensive simulator or experiment. This page expands the Active Learning section of the surrogate roadmap into a design and a delivery plan. Citations (AL1–AL22, MF*) refer to the tables on the References page.

Goals and Non-Goals¶

Goals

  1. Global surrogate accuracy is the main use case: spend a fixed budget of simulator runs so the fitted surrogate is accurate across the whole input domain, for every output (AL20, AL21, AL22).

  2. Optimization of one output, or of a weighted sum of outputs, through the classical Bayesian optimization criteria (EI, PI, UCB) (AL2, AL3, AL4).

  3. Multi-fidelity problems: choose both where to sample and at which fidelity level, trading information against cost (see Multi-Fidelity Active Learning).

  4. Work with any surrogate in the package, not only GPs, by falling back to committee disagreement when a model has no predictive std.

  5. A simulator-agnostic loop: an ask / tell interface for simulators that run outside Python (HPC queues, lab experiments), and a run helper for Python callables.

Non-goals (for now)

  • Replacing BoTorch or emukit. Gradient-based acquisition optimization, Monte-Carlo batch acquisitions (qEI, qNEI) and knowledge-gradient methods are out of scope; acquisitions are scored on a finite candidate pool.

  • Multi-objective optimization (hypervolume improvement, ParEGO).

  • Constrained optimization and categorical inputs.

Current State¶

multioutreg does not do active learning. Every fitting path (AutoDetectMultiOutputRegressor.fit, the grid-search CLIs, the GUI pages and the multi-fidelity surrogates) fits once on a fixed dataset. The multioutreg.sampling module only infers which design a dataset came from (Random, LHS or Sobol).

Architecture¶

A new package, multioutreg/active/:

Module

Contents

acquisition.py

Uncertainty extraction (predict_mean_std, CommitteeSurrogate), output standardization and aggregation, and acquisition functions on (mean, std) arrays: max_variance, upper_confidence_bound, expected_improvement, probability_of_improvement

batch.py

select_batch: greedy top-k with a distance penalty around already-chosen points (local penalization / maximin style)

loop.py

ActiveLearner: initial design, candidate pool, ask / tell / run, history and stopping rules

multi_fidelity.py

Per-level variance reduction (fidelity_info), level trust, cost-aware acquisitions, and MultiFidelityActiveLearner

benchmarks.py

Analytic test functions, including standard multi-fidelity pairs, for tests, docs and the tutorial notebook

Conventions¶

  • Shapes. Every acquisition takes mean and std arrays of shape (n_candidates, n_outputs) and returns a score of shape (n_candidates,); larger is better. Acquisitions are plain functions, so they can be used without the loop.

  • Inputs live in a box. The learner takes bounds of shape (n_features, 2). Candidate pools are drawn in the unit cube and scaled; distances for batch diversity are measured in the unit cube, so input units do not matter.

  • Model factory. The learner takes a zero-argument callable that returns an unfitted surrogate, and refits from scratch each iteration. That is simpler and safer than warm starts, and the fit cost is negligible next to an expensive simulator.

Uncertainty Sources¶

  1. Native std: model.predict(X, return_std=True).

  2. Committee (query-by-committee, AL7, AL8): CommitteeSurrogate wraps any model factory, fits n_members copies on bootstrap resamples, and reports the mean and the standard deviation across members. This brings KNN, linear models and plain trees into active learning.

  3. Conformal width (later): half-width of normalized split-conformal intervals as the score, for a coverage guarantee on the uncertainty itself.

Zero std is not certainty. BaseSurrogate.predict(return_std=True) and AutoDetectMultiOutputRegressor.predict return zeros for an output whose model has no native uncertainty. A variance-based acquisition would treat that output as perfectly known and never sample for it. predict_mean_std raises when an output’s std is identically zero and points to CommitteeSurrogate. Changing AutoDetectMultiOutputRegressor to return NaN there is tracked separately; it changes a public return value.

Multi-Output Aggregation¶

Raw stds summed across outputs let the largest-scale output dominate. Each output’s std is divided by the standard deviation of that output’s observed training values, then combined with "sum" (default; total standardized variance), "max" (the worst-known output drives sampling), or "mean". Optional per-output weights express priorities between outputs.

For the optimization criteria, outputs are first scalarized with weights (g = Y @ w), and the std of g is sqrt(sum w_j^2 std_j^2), which assumes independent outputs. Per-output surrogates are independent by construction, so this matches what the models actually say.

Optimization (AL4)

Integrated-variance reduction (ALC, AL6) is the better global-accuracy criterion, because it rewards points that reduce uncertainty in their neighbourhood rather than only at themselves. It needs a joint posterior covariance, so it comes after the GP-specific helpers in phase 3.

Batch Selection¶

With batch size q > 1, taking the top q scores clusters the batch on one ridge of the acquisition surface. select_batch picks greedily: after each pick, every remaining candidate’s score is multiplied by 1 - exp(-d^2 / (2 l^2)), where d is the unit-cube distance to the pick (AL10, AL11, AL12). The default l is the typical spacing of a space-filling design of n_train + q points, (n_train + q)^(-1/d). Training points can be penalized as well (penalize_train=True) for models whose std does not shrink near data (committees of trees).

The Loop¶

initial design (scrambled Sobol or LHS, n_init points)
  └─► evaluate simulator ─► fit surrogate ─► draw candidate pool (fresh Sobol each iteration)
        ▲                                         │
        │                                         ▼
        └──── tell(X_new, Y_new) ◄── ask(q): score pool, select_batch

  • ask(n) returns the next n points without evaluating anything; tell(X, Y) appends results and marks the model stale. run(simulator, n_iter, ...) chains them for Python simulators.

  • History: one record per fitted model (number of evaluations, best observed objective, and held-out RMSE / NRMSE when test data is supplied) for convergence plots.

  • Stopping rules: budget (max_evals), and held-out NRMSE not improving by a relative rtol for patience iterations. A threshold on the maximum standardized std over the pool (no test data needed) comes later.

  • Reproducibility: one random_state seeds the initial design and every candidate pool.

Multi-Fidelity Active Learning¶

With several fidelity levels, each query is a pair (x, level). A low-fidelity run is cheap but only partly informative about the high-fidelity response; a high-fidelity run is fully informative but expensive. The aim is the most accurate high-fidelity surrogate per unit of total cost.

Problem Setup¶

  • Levels are ordered cheapest to most expensive, l = 0, ..., L-1, with known costs c_l per evaluation (wall-clock or core-hours), given by the user. Cost depending on x (mesh size, convergence) is out of scope for the first version.

  • One simulator per level: simulators = {"lo": f_lo, "hi": f_hi}.

  • A total budget B in cost units. Accuracy is tracked against cumulative cost, not against the number of samples.

  • The surrogate is any multi-fidelity model in the package: AdditiveCorrectionVFM (two levels) or StackedVFMSurrogate (any number).

Information Per Level¶

The acquisition needs to know how much a run at level l and point x would reduce uncertainty about f_hi(x).

AR1 models (AdditiveCorrectionVFM). The model is f_hi(x) = rho · f_lo(x) + delta(x), so the high-fidelity variance splits into two parts:

var_hi(x) = rho^2 · var_lo(x) + var_delta(x)

  • A low-fidelity run at x can only reduce the first term: R_lo(x) = rho^2 · var_lo(x).

  • A high-fidelity run updates delta. The AR1 fit needs f_lo at the high-fidelity points, so by default a high-fidelity query also runs the low fidelity at the same x (a nested design, which also suits the out-of-fold logic in _OOFPredictor). It then reduces both terms, R_hi(x) = rho^2 · var_lo(x) + var_delta(x), and costs c_hi + c_lo. With nested=False it reduces only var_delta(x) and costs c_hi.

When the model is right, this decomposition corrects itself: once the low-fidelity model is well sampled, var_lo is small everywhere, R_lo falls towards zero, and the remaining var_delta, which only high-fidelity runs can reduce, takes over.

It is not safe on its own. The prototype showed the failure. With a low fidelity that is pure noise and 3 high-fidelity points, least squares fits rho ≈ ±300. A GP on noise keeps a large var_lo, so rho^2 · var_lo looks valuable everywhere, and the learner spent its entire budget on the noise. No further high-fidelity point ever arrived to correct rho. Hence the level trust safeguard below.

Other models (StackedVFMSurrogate and anything with predict(X, level=...)). There is no analytic split, so the plan follows the correlation factor of multi-fidelity EI (Huang et al. 2006, AL16):

R_l(x) = corr_l^2 · var_hi(x),   corr_{L-1} = 1

where corr_l is the correlation between level-l and high-fidelity predictions over the candidate pool (one value per output, clipped to [0, 1]). This is cruder: it does not change with x; in a stacked model the high level is built on the lower one’s predictions, so the two correlate by construction (the trust factor below is what keeps this honest); and StackedVFMSurrogate reports only the top level’s own std (see its docstring). It is the fallback, not the main path.

C_l is the effective cost of a query at level l (c_hi + c_lo for a nested high-fidelity query). The learner scores every (x, l) pair in the pool and takes the best. For MF-EI with AR1 models, corr_l(x) is rho · std_lo(x) / std_hi(x) for the low level and 1 for the high level.

Safeguards¶

  • Seeding: each level starts with its own space-filling design; high-fidelity seed points are a subset of the low-fidelity ones, so the initial design is nested.

  • Level trust (level_trust, trust_z=1.0): each lower level’s reduction is multiplied by max(0, LCB(|r|))^2, where r is the correlation between that level’s prediction and the observed high-fidelity values at the high-fidelity points, and LCB is a Fisher-z lower bound tanh(atanh(|r|) - z / sqrt(n - 3)). Trust is 0 below 6 high-fidelity points: with 4 points pure noise reached |r| = 0.97 in the prototype’s tests. A lower level earns credit only once the data shows it predicts the high fidelity, whatever the fitted model claims.

  • Minimum high-fidelity share (min_hi_fraction, default 0.25): forces a high-fidelity query whenever high-fidelity spending falls below that share of the total spent so far. Evidence about a lower level only comes from high-fidelity runs, so without this a spuriously trusted level can lock in. It also guards against a low fidelity that is overconfident (small var_lo) but biased.

  • Budget overrun: a query whose cost exceeds the remaining budget is skipped in favour of the best affordable one; the loop stops when nothing is affordable.

  • Evaluation: accuracy vs cumulative cost against two baselines: random high-fidelity sampling, and single-fidelity active learning on the high level alone. Report the share of the budget spent at each level.

Open Questions¶

  • Dropping a useless level from the model. The safeguards protect the spending, not the surrogate. In the tutorial’s pure-noise experiment the learner put about 85% of the budget into the high fidelity, yet AdditiveCorrectionVFM still pushed the noise through rho · f_lo and was far worse than a GP on the same high-fidelity points alone. The learner should compare the fused model against a high-fidelity-only model on held-out or cross-validated high-fidelity error, and drop levels whose trust stays near zero.

  • Pointwise reduction ignores that a run at x also informs nearby points. An integrated (ALC-style) multi-fidelity criterion needs joint covariances per level, which only the GP surrogates provide.

  • Batches that mix levels (for example 1 high-fidelity run plus 10 low-fidelity runs per HPC allocation) need a batch-cost model; the prototype picks one query at a time.

  • StackedVFMSurrogate should propagate lower-level uncertainty (Monte Carlo through the augmented features) before its std is used for acquisition; until then the correlation fallback is used.

  • Fidelity as a continuous input (roadmap MF item 3) would turn the level choice into a search over a continuous cost dial.

Testing Plan¶

  • Unit: acquisition values on hand-computed cases (EI at std = 0, UCB sign under minimize, PI bounds), standardization invariance (scaling one output does not change max_variance), zero-std detection, batch diversity (no two picks closer than l / 2 on a flat score), and benchmark values at known optima (Branin, Hartmann-3).

  • Loop: ask / tell round-trip shapes; the budget is respected; reproducible with a fixed seed; active learning beats random sampling on Forrester with a GP at a fixed budget (seeded, loose margin).

  • Multi-fidelity: costs are accounted exactly; designs stay nested; min_hi_fraction holds; the AR1 variance split adds up to var_hi; trust is 0 below 6 points, near 1 for an exact low fidelity and small for noise; with a pure-noise low level the learner keeps buying high-fidelity runs; with a perfectly correlated, 20x cheaper low level it prefers low-fidelity runs.

Risks¶

  • Model refit cost. Refitting a GP every iteration is O(n^3); past a few hundred points, use a sparse GP or a larger batch.

  • Miscalibrated std. Acquisitions trust the std they are given. Tree ensembles and deep ensembles are often overconfident away from data; the diagnostics (calibration plots in figures/uct_calibration_extension.py) should be checked on the final design.

  • Adaptive designs fail the sampling detector. An actively built design is neither random, LHS nor Sobol and will be reported as “Uncertain”; reports must say the design was built adaptively.
